## Prompt:
I'm having trouble with an agent who can start a large refactoring and do ~80% of it but really struggles to get to 95% and then it's like trying to get a horse to walk down a flight of stairs to actually complete the last little bit.  And it is always trying to 'defer' the challenging work to later sprints, even when an entire project is blocked on it

## Response:
What you are describing is not a personality flaw in the agent.

It is a **phase-transition failure** in how transformers handle risk, scope, and reward.

And once you see it, you can fix it.

* * *

Why 80% feels easy and 95% feels impossible
-------------------------------------------

Large refactors have two different kinds of work:

### Phase 1 — _Expansive, low-risk_

*   Moving files
*   Renaming symbols
*   Updating imports
*   Mechanical edits
*   “Obvious” transformations

These have:

*   High pattern similarity
*   Low ambiguity
*   Low chance of catastrophic breakage

Transformers love this.  
They can do it almost indefinitely.

* * *

### Phase 2 — _Convergent, high-risk_

This is where you hit:

*   Edge cases
*   Cross-cutting invariants
*   Data model inconsistencies
*   Migration logic
*   Backward compatibility
*   Tests that no longer compile

Here, every change risks breaking something.

Now the model’s internal objectives conflict:

*   “Make progress”
*   “Don’t introduce errors”
*   “Don’t hallucinate”
*   “Don’t break tests”
*   “Don’t touch unknowns”

The model does not know how to trade these off, so it tries to escape the conflict by **deferring**.

> “We should handle this later.”  
> “This is out of scope.”  
> “We should add a TODO.”

That is not laziness.  
That is **uncertainty collapse**.

* * *

Why it feels like a horse refusing stairs
-----------------------------------------

Stairs require:

*   Precise footing
*   No sweeping motion
*   One wrong step = injury

Transformers are trained on **broad continuation**, not **narrow risk navigation**.

They are amazing at highways.  
They are terrible at staircases.

* * *

The fix: force a phase change
-----------------------------

You must explicitly tell the agent:

> _We are no longer optimizing for speed. We are now optimizing for closure._

This is not motivational.  
This changes what the model thinks the task _is_.

You do this by activating a **Convergence Mode**.
